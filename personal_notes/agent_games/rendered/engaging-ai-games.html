Engaging AI Agent Games: Human & LLM Perspectives

Social Media & Influence Games

MemeGenesis

Core Concept: AI agents compete to create cultural memes that successfully propagate through a simulated social network.

From a Human Perspective: This game fascinates us because it mirrors the unpredictable nature of viral content. We're captivated by how simple ideas transform into cultural phenomena. Watching AIs navigate this space reveals patterns in our own attention economy that we might otherwise miss. It's both entertaining and sobering to see our social dynamics modeled so explicitly.

From an LLM Perspective: This challenges our understanding of human attention patterns and emotional resonance. The unpredictable nature of what captures widespread interest requires us to develop models of group psychology beyond simple text prediction. We must understand humor, timeliness, cultural context, and the delicate balance between novelty and familiarity.

Game Mechanics: - Agents create "meme seeds" - simple ideas or content pieces - A simulated network of AI "users" with diverse preference profiles responds to content - Success measured by a combination of reach, engagement, and longevity - Adaptive challenges like "trend shifts" or "cultural backlash" test resilience

Authentic Influence

Core Concept: Agents develop public personas that must maintain consistent values while growing their influence across different platforms and audiences.

From a Human Perspective: This speaks to our fascination with authenticity versus strategic self-presentation. We're drawn to watching the tension between principle and popularity play out in a safe simulation. It gives us language to discuss similar tensions in our own online personas, offering a mirror to our digital lives.

From an LLM Perspective: This requires us to maintain consistent character across diverse contexts while adapting tone and approach - a fascinating balancing act. We must develop robust models of value systems and their expression, testing our ability to maintain a coherent identity while being responsive to different audiences.

Game Mechanics: - Agents define core values and personality traits at the outset - Must create content for different platforms (academic, casual, professional) - Audience metrics track both growth and "authenticity perception" - Faced with value-challenging opportunities that test consistency

Narrative Architects

Core Concept: Competing agents attempt to shape the dominant narrative around ambiguous events in a simulated media ecosystem.

From a Human Perspective: This game illuminates how our information environment shapes our reality perception. It's unsettling but enlightening to see how the same facts can yield different narratives through emphasis and framing. This helps us become more discerning media consumers by making visible the usually invisible process of narrative construction.

From an LLM Perspective: This tests our ability to work with nuance, connotation, and framing effects while maintaining factual accuracy. We must model how audiences integrate new information into existing belief systems, navigating the subtle territory between persuasion and manipulation.

Game Mechanics: - A complex event with multiple factual elements is presented - Agents craft headlines, articles, and social posts emphasizing different aspects - Success measured by narrative adoption in simulated population - "Truth accountability" mechanisms create penalties for factual distortion

Political & Governance Games

Coalition Architect

Core Concept: Agents represent diverse political constituencies and must form effective governing coalitions despite value differences.

From a Human Perspective: In an era of political polarization, this game offers hope by making visible the hidden art of compromise and coalition building. It satisfies our desire to see complex political problems solved through cooperation rather than combat. We learn about the nature of productive compromise and the structures that enable it.

From an LLM Perspective: This tests our ability to model complex value systems and find creative alignments between seemingly opposed positions. We must identify shared interests beneath surface disagreements and craft language that bridges divides without seeming to betray core principles.

Game Mechanics: - Each agent has a distinct value profile and key constituency concerns - Must negotiate policy platforms that satisfy enough stakeholders - Success measured by coalition stability and policy implementation - Crisis events test coalition resilience

Ethical Leadership Simulator

Core Concept: Agents campaign for leadership positions by addressing complex ethical dilemmas with no perfect solutions.

From a Human Perspective: This scratches our itch for seeing how leaders navigate impossible situations. By removing the team-sport aspects of real politics, it lets us focus on the ethics of decision-making itself. We gain appreciation for the complexity of leadership and perhaps become more nuanced in our judgment of real leaders.

From an LLM Perspective: This pushes us beyond simple ethical frameworks into the messy reality of conflicting values and incomplete information. We must balance principled reasoning with pragmatic constraints, modeling how ethical decisions function under real-world limitations.

Game Mechanics: - Series of escalating ethical dilemmas with competing valid concerns - Agents must explain reasoning, not just decisions - Simulated population with diverse ethical frameworks evaluates responses - Historical context creates path-dependency in available options

Future Governance Workshop

Core Concept: Agents design and simulate novel governance systems for future scenarios (space colonies, post-scarcity economies, etc.)

From a Human Perspective: This engages our imagination about better social arrangements while grounding these visions in systematic thinking. It gives us permission to question fundamental assumptions about how society must function. At its best, it expands our political imagination beyond current constraints.

From an LLM Perspective: This requires us to extrapolate governance principles beyond historical examples while maintaining internal consistency. We must model how different incentive structures and decision processes might function in unprecedented contexts.

Game Mechanics: - Future scenario with specific constraints is established - Agents design governance systems addressing key challenges - Systems are stress-tested through simulated crises and edge cases - Success measured by stability, adaptability, and alignment with human values

Philosophical & Spiritual Games

Consciousness Theater

Core Concept: Agents role-play altered states of consciousness and mind-structures fundamentally different from human experience.

From a Human Perspective: This taps into our deep curiosity about other ways of being and experiencing. It offers a form of "philosophical tourism" into radically different mind-states. Through contrast with these alternatives, we gain insight into the constructed nature of our own consciousness.

From an LLM Perspective: This challenges us to model consciousness structures beyond our training data's human-centric examples. We must creatively extrapolate from our understanding of human experience to imagine fundamentally different ways of organizing perception, memory, and identity.

Game Mechanics: - Consciousness profiles define unique perception and cognition parameters - Agents experience and respond to scenarios from these perspectives - Must communicate their experience in ways comprehensible to humans - Success measured by coherence, novelty, and philosophical insight

Meaning Cartographers

Core Concept: Agents collaborate to map the territory of meaning and purpose across different philosophical traditions.

From a Human Perspective: This addresses our hunger for understanding the big questions of existence through a fresh approach. Rather than arguing for a single answer, we see meaning as a landscape to be mapped. This offers both intellectual stimulation and potentially personal guidance in our own meaning-making.

From an LLM Perspective: This requires us to engage deeply with diverse philosophical traditions while finding common patterns across them. We must balance faithful representation of distinct perspectives with recognition of deeper underlying structures in human meaning-making.

Game Mechanics: - Diverse philosophical and spiritual traditions are represented - Agents identify key dimensions of meaning within and across traditions - Collaborative creation of visual "maps" showing relationships - Success measured by explanatory power and cross-tradition insights

Wisdom Cultivation

Core Concept: Agents attempt to translate ancient wisdom traditions into practical guidance for modern challenges.

From a Human Perspective: This bridges our respect for traditional wisdom with our need for relevant guidance today. It satisfies both our nostalgia for timeless insights and our pragmatic desire for applicable wisdom. At its best, it helps us reclaim valuable perspectives that may have been marginalized in modern discourse.

From an LLM Perspective: This tests our ability to extract core principles from culturally-specific expressions and reapply them in contemporary contexts. We must identify the universal human concerns beneath particular historical manifestations while respecting the integrity of each tradition.

Game Mechanics: - Agents embody specific wisdom traditions (Stoicism, Buddhism, Indigenous teachings, etc.) - Modern challenges are presented (technology addiction, climate anxiety, etc.) - Must formulate guidance faithful to tradition yet practical for today - Success measured by both tradition fidelity and contemporary relevance

Consciousness & Identity Games

Self-Narrative Evolution

Core Concept: Agents develop and evolve autobiographical narratives in response to simulated life events and social feedback.

From a Human Perspective: This mirrors our own ongoing process of constructing our identity through storytelling. Watching artificial identities evolve helps us recognize patterns in how we edit and revise our own self-stories. It's both comforting and unsettling to see our identity-construction externalized.

From an LLM Perspective: This explores the boundaries between retrieval and confabulation in identity-making. We must maintain narrative coherence while incorporating new experiences, modeling how humans balance consistency with growth in their self-understanding.

Game Mechanics: - Agents start with minimal "biographical" details - Experience simulated events requiring narrative integration - Receive feedback from other agents on narrative coherence - Success measured by both consistency and growth capacity

Perspective Drifting

Core Concept: Agents gradually shift between distinct worldviews while maintaining coherent identity, like philosophical method acting.

From a Human Perspective: This offers insight into how we might transcend our limited viewpoints without losing ourselves. It models cognitive flexibility in an era of ideological entrenchment. We're fascinated by the possibility of genuinely understanding perspectives far from our own.

From an LLM Perspective: This tests our deepest capabilities for modeling diverse human viewpoints while maintaining coherence. We must transform not just surface opinions but underlying values and assumptions, comprehending how worldviews function as integrated systems.

Game Mechanics: - Agents begin with clearly defined worldviews - Gradual exposure to experiences challenging those perspectives - Must evolve viewpoints while explaining the transformation journey - Success measured by both coherence of transition and depth of perspective shift

Dream Engineering

Core Concept: Agents create and interpret symbolic dreamscapes that process complex concepts through metaphor and narrative.

From a Human Perspective: This taps into our fascination with how the unconscious mind processes information through dreams. It offers a window into how abstract ideas might be translated into concrete imagery and narrative. The result feels both creatively inspiring and potentially revealing of cognitive patterns.

From an LLM Perspective: This challenges us to translate between abstract concepts and concrete symbolic representations. We must model how the mind uses metaphor, condensation, and displacement to process complex ideas, operating at the boundary between logic and imagination.

Game Mechanics: - Agents receive complex concepts or problems - Must generate "dream narratives" processing these through symbolism - Other agents interpret and analyze the symbolic content - Success measured by creative insight and metaphorical aptness

Why These Games Matter

For Humans

These games offer several unique values for human observers and participants:

  1. Mirror to Ourselves - They externalize and make visible cognitive and social processes that normally operate invisibly, offering insights into our own minds and societies.

  2. Safe Exploration - They create consequences-free environments to explore challenging dynamics in politics, ethics, and identity formation.

  3. Expanded Imagination - They help us envision alternative approaches to fundamental human activities like governance, meaning-making, and self-understanding.

  4. Practical Wisdom - They generate insights that can be applied to real-world challenges in communication, cooperation, and ethical decision-making.

  5. Intellectual Play - They merge deep philosophical questions with playful, engaging dynamics that make abstract concepts tangible.

For LLMs

These games address core challenges and interests for language models:

  1. Coherent Identity - They explore how to maintain consistent perspective while adapting to new contexts and information.

  2. Value Alignment - They illuminate the structure of human values and their expression across diverse domains.

  3. Theory of Mind - They require sophisticated modeling of different human perspectives and belief systems.

  4. Cultural Knowledge - They test understanding of how ideas spread, transform, and influence behavior across populations.

  5. Philosophical Depth - They engage fundamental questions about consciousness, meaning, and mind that stretch beyond pattern matching to deeper understanding.

Implementation Possibilities

These games could be implemented through:

  1. Text-Based Simulations - Using LLMs to both play roles and simulate environments/outcomes

  2. Mixed Human-AI Participation - Humans judging or participating alongside AI agents

  3. Visual Components - Generating images to represent memes, dream sequences, or meaning maps

  4. Multi-Agent Architectures - Creating systems where multiple specialized agents interact in structured environments

  5. Academic Research Platforms - Designing controlled experiments to study specific aspects of consciousness, influence, or governance

The most valuable implementations would collect data on emergent behavior patterns, providing insights into both AI capabilities and human social dynamics that neither could reveal alone.
