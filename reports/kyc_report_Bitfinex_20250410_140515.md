## Executive Summary

Bitfinex, a prominent cryptocurrency exchange platform, presents significant risk factors leading to its categorization under RiskCategory.PROHIBITED. A comprehensive assessment reveals key concerns regarding its operational transparency and regulatory compliance. As part of its business model, Bitfinex facilitates high-frequency trading of digital assets, a sector marked by elevated volatility and susceptibility to financial crimes such as money laundering and fraud. Additionally, the company has faced scrutiny over its past handling of customer data privacy and the security of digital funds, exacerbated by previous allegations and regulatory investigations. 

Despite Bitfinex’s innovative contributions to the cryptocurrency marketplace, the inherent risks associated with cryptocurrency exchanges, particularly in jurisdictions with evolving regulatory frameworks, elevate the risk profile beyond acceptable thresholds. These factors, compounded by the absence of robust safeguarding measures against financial misconduct, lead to an overall risk determination that falls under the category of prohibited engagement according to our risk appetite.

In conclusion, due to the potential compliance challenges and unresolved regulatory issues, it is recommended to refrain from proceeding with any business engagements with Bitfinex to mitigate exposure to potential financial and reputational risk.

# Regulatory Compliance and Licensing Risk Assessment

## Key Findings from Research

Bitfinex has encountered regulatory challenges, particularly in the UK jurisdiction. According to the latest statement from the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) dated October 29, 2023, Bitfinex is on the FCA's warning list of unauthorized firms. This signals regulatory non-compliance issues with the FCA's standards (source: [FCA Statement](https://blog.bitfinex.com/announcements/fca-statement-2/)). Furthermore, Bitfinex has responded by restricting access to several services for UK-based customers and removing product availability in the UK, effective from January 1, 2024 (source: [Notice to UK Customers](https://blog.bitfinex.com/announcements/notice-to-uk-customers/)).

In contrast, Bitfinex is successfully licensed as a Digital Asset Service Provider in El Salvador, indicating strong regulatory compliance within this jurisdiction. This is demonstrated by their first-mover advantage in obtaining licensing under El Salvador's Digital Asset Issuance Law (source: [Bitfinex Securities in El Salvador](https://blog.bitfinex.com/bitfinex-securities/bitfinex-securities-launches-express-onboarding-for-salvadoran-residents/)).

## Identified Risk Factors

- **UK Jurisdiction**: Bitfinex's placement on the FCA warning list poses a significant risk in terms of regulatory compliance in the UK. The inability to secure authorization from the FCA may imply lapses in adherence to local financial regulations, which historically can characterize financial crime risks.
- **License Exposure**: While licensed successfully in El Salvador, Bitfinex's exposure to regulatory environments is limited to certain jurisdictions, with compliance challenges persisting elsewhere.

## Relevance to Company’s Risk Appetite

Corpay's risk appetite includes prohibitions against unauthorized, unlicensed, or unregulated exchanges, which directly applies to the current FCA situation. The inability to comply with UK regulatory standards may suggest potential intentional or negligent breaches of law, contrary to Corpay's policies. Bitfinex’s limited licensed operations in only select jurisdictions (El Salvador) and its disregard in the UK aligns with Corpay's in-house restrictions concerning Virtual Asset Service Providers. Enhanced due diligence is warranted.

# AML/KYC Practices: Bitfinex

## Key Findings from Research
Bitfinex operates as a cryptocurrency exchange platform, adhering to certain AML and KYC practices. According to information from **GetID** [source](https://getid.com/aml-kyc-crypto-exchanges-wallets/), Bitfinex has a unique approach to KYC requirements, distinguishing between crypto-only users and users handling fiat currencies. Users who strictly engage in crypto transactions are not required to undergo KYC verification processes. However, users who wish to deposit or trade fiat currencies must provide comprehensive identification, including an address, phone number, proof of address, and two forms of government-issued identification. This approach raises considerations about their alignment with traditional financial institutions' compliance requirements.

The platform's practices need to be evaluated in the context of evolving regulatory frameworks, such as the CANSEE Act introduced by Sen. Jack Reed [source](https://blog.bitfinex.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Bitfinex-Alpha-64.pdf), which emphasizes stricter AML and KYC regulations for decentralized finance platforms.

## Identified Risk Factors
1. **Differentiated KYC Practices**: Bitfinex's policy of allowing crypto-only users to bypass KYC processes presents risks related to anonymity, which could potentially facilitate money laundering or other illicit activities.
   
2. **Regulatory Changes**: With ongoing legislative developments like the CANSEE Act, there is potential exposure to increased regulatory scrutiny if Bitfinex's practices are perceived not to align with the tightened KYC and AML requirements.

3. **Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) Classification**: Bitfinex inherently faces the typical risks associated with VASPs, including security of funds, transparency in operations, and adherence to international standards.

## Relevance to Company's Risk Appetite
Corpay's risk appetite statement clearly articulates a disinterest in engaging with anonymous, unregistered, or unregulated virtual currency exchanges, prioritizing transparency and compliance. Bitfinex's practice of allowing anonymity for crypto-only users might conflict with Corpay’s prohibitions against such entities. Therefore, handling business transactions with Bitfinex could fall outside the scope of Corpay’s appetite, especially considering stringent requirements for identifying and verifying customer identities.

## Supporting Evidence and Sources
- **GetID**: Highlights the distinctive approach of Bitfinex in handling KYC procedures, especially regarding the segregation of crypto-only and fiat-supported transactions.
- **Bitfinex Blog**: Provides insights into the legislative environment, including the CANSEE Act, that could impact Bitfinex's operations in the DeFi sector.

## Conclusion
Bitfinex's AML/KYC practices present distinct risk factors due to their differentiated approach towards identity verification based on transaction types, especially in light of regulatory advancements requiring stronger oversight. For Corpay, engaging with Bitfinex may propose challenges, primarily due to potential incompatibilities with its risk appetite framework, focusing on transparent operations and compliance with financial crime regulations. It is essential for Corpay to conduct enhanced due diligence to ensure alignment with its compliance standard and regulatory necessities when considering business interactions with entities in the cryptocurrency sector.

# Negative News and Regulatory Actions

The breach subsequently caused a major decline in bitcoin's trading price. In February 2022, federal authorities charged a couple with laundering the stolen bitcoins, now valued at $5.3 billion. This indicates significant vulnerabilities in Bitfinex's security measures at that time. [Source: Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Bitfinex_hack)

2. **Tether Connection and Financial Cover-up**: Bitfinex, which shares a parent company with Tether (iFinex), was accused by the New York Attorney General's office in 2019 of using Tether's funds as collateral to cover an alleged $850 million loss. This arose from severe issues Bitfinex faced with client withdrawal requests. The accusation points to possible misappropriation of funds and lack of transparency. Tether had also failed to produce a promised audit of its accounts, leading to further scrutiny from regulators including subpoenas from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) in December 2017. ## Technical and Cybersecurity Risk Assessment for Bitfinex

### Key Findings from Research

Bitfinex has shown a strong commitment to ensuring cybersecurity and safeguarding its virtual asset services. According to their announcements and reports:

- **SOC 2 Compliance**: Bitfinex has completed a SOC 2 Type 1 compliance audit and is planning to obtain SOC 2 Type 2 compliance in 2023. The SOC 2 standards evaluate the effectiveness of data security controls, highlighting Bitfinex's dedication to maintaining high security measures (source: [Bitfinex Blog](https://blog.bitfinex.com/announcements/bitfinex-has-completed-the-soc-2-type-1-compliance/)).
- **Security Features**: They offer multiple security features such as hot and cold wallets, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) protection, user-driven security measures including two-factor authentication (2FA), account verification, withdrawal protection, and regular audits to detect and block unauthorized access (source: [news.zke.com](https://news.zke.com/enhancing-security-for-bitfinex-users-comprehensive-checklist-released), [VPNSuper](https://www.vpnsuper.com/blog/is-bitfinex-safe)).
- **Cold Storage Solutions**: Approximately 99.5% of user funds are stored in offline multisignature wallets, reducing exposure to online threats, while the remaining 0.5% are kept in hot wallets for daily operations. Multi-signature verification is required for transaction approvals (source: [news.zke.com](https://news.zke.com/bitfinex-highlights-enhanced-security-measures-for-crypto-traders/)).

### Identified Risk Factors

Despite the robust security measures, there remain inherent risks associated with virtual asset service providers like Bitfinex:

- **Cyber Threat Exposure**: As with any online platform, Bitfinex is exposed to potential cyber threats, including phishing attacks and data breaches. Continuous vigilance and regular system audits are critical.
- **Compliance Gaps**: While SOC 2 Type 1 certification has been achieved, ongoing SOC 2 Type 2 compliance reviews are necessary to ensure continuous adaptation to evolving cyber threats.

### Relevance to Company's Risk Appetite

Bitfinex is categorized under Virtual Asset Service Providers, which is identified within Corpay's risk appetite statement as presenting heightened concerns requiring enhanced due diligence. The company has asserted a no tolerance policy towards unlicensed and unregulated exchanges, ensuring compliance with industry standards and legal regulations. 

The proactive measures Bitfinex has taken align with the requirement for security and compliance, although ongoing verification and updates on their compliance status are essential given the dynamic nature of cybersecurity threats.
